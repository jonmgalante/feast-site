Feast Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 13, 2026

Feast is designed to help you save and organize places. This page explains, in plain language, how Feast handles information.

Your saved collection

Feast does not require a separate account to save and organize places. Your cities, neighborhoods, places, notes and other saved details are stored on your device and in your configured Apple iCloud stores. If you share a city, its content is available to the people you invite through Apple’s sharing tools. Ask Feast does not replace those stores or shares, but enabling online AI allows the information described below to be sent to external services.

When someone with access to a shared city enables online Ask, relevant shared content, including notes contributed by others, may be processed by AI and web services as described below. Turning off online AI on your device does not change another participant’s access or Ask settings.

Feast uses Apple Maps for place search and map information. If you allow location access, your location helps position the map and find nearby places. Device location is not automatically included in Ask Feast requests.

Optional Ask Feast access and permission

Online Ask Feast requires an invitation for your device. Activation sends the invitation code, a random device access identifier, an activation identifier and a hash of a device credential to Feast’s service hosted by Cloudflare. The service keeps hashes and an activation receipt to authenticate access, prevent reuse and support revocation. It does not receive your saved collection during activation.

If you manage invitations, Feast stores the name or label you enter to identify invited devices.

Activation does not grant permission to send AI requests. Before your first online question, Feast explains what will be shared and asks you to allow it. You can decline and continue using the collection, Maps and sharing features.

What goes to AI and web services

With your permission, your questions, a compact saved-place index, relevant collection details and limited recent conversation context, including findings and place references, pass through Cloudflare to OpenAI to generate answers, research places and interpret supported app actions. Collection details can include place names, cities, neighborhoods, categories, cuisines, statuses, tags, notes and saved website or Instagram links. This includes notes other people added to shared collections you can access. Ask uses collections you already have permission to access; enabling it does not grant access to other collections. Unselected photos are not sent.

For web research, OpenAI may use your questions and relevant collection or conversation context, including saved and shared note details, to form queries sent to search providers or public sites. Public pages and business websites may also be retrieved to verify information. These recipients may log or retain queries under their own privacy practices. This also applies to personal information you choose to type into a question.

The current online release does not enable image uploads.

Ask can make supported collection changes when you instruct it or use a Save action. It asks for clarification when necessary, and available Undo actions are shown after persistence. These changes use Feast’s existing storage and sharing permissions.

Storage and retention

Feast keeps conversations, findings and action outcomes on your device so you can return to earlier chats. Device backups may include app data according to your Apple backup settings.

Online answer recovery is disabled in the current release. Feast does not currently store request and answer copies on Cloudflare for later recovery. Leaving Ask Feast may interrupt an answer. A request already sent to an external service may still finish and be processed under that service’s retention practices.

Feast’s Cloudflare backend retains access and request identifiers, credential hashes, request-state and diagnostic codes, and usage information for authentication, duplicate prevention, security and provider usage accounting. Access and usage records, including revoked access and unresolved requests, do not currently expire automatically. They are kept as needed for these purposes; contact us about access or deletion requests.

Feast sends OpenAI requests with response storage disabled. This does not mean that all provider retention is disabled. Under OpenAI’s standard API policy, abuse-monitoring logs may contain collection details and other request content as well as answers. These logs are normally retained for up to 30 days, with longer retention for legal or safety reasons. Other service processing and caching policies may apply. Feast has not enabled sharing API inputs and outputs for model training.

Read OpenAI’s data controls, Cloudflare’s privacy policy and Apple’s privacy policy for their practices. Public websites and search services have their own privacy practices.

Your choices

You can turn off Allow online AI in Feast’s settings to prevent new AI requests. Withdrawal cannot recall information already submitted. You can delete local conversations using History controls. Revoking Ask access for a device does not delete its saved collection or change iCloud sharing.

Feast does not sell your data, use third-party advertising trackers, or include third-party analytics SDKs. Service providers process the information described here to deliver and protect the features you use.

Support and privacy requests

If you contact support, we use your email and the information you send to respond and resolve the issue. You can also contact us to ask about retained data, request access or deletion, or revoke Ask access for a lost device. We may need to verify the request and retain information required for security, unresolved usage or legal obligations.

Do not send invitation codes, credentials or sensitive notes in a support message.
